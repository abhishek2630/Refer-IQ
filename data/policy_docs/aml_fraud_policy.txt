SECTION 1: POCA 2002 — Proceeds of Crime Act Obligations

1.1 SAR SUBMISSION THRESHOLD: Under POCA 2002 s.330, a firm must submit a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) to the National Crime Agency (NCA) where a person in the regulated sector knows or suspects, or has reasonable grounds to know or suspect, that another person is engaged in money laundering.

1.2 The threshold for SAR submission is suspicion, not proof. Where a firm has reasonable grounds to suspect that funds may be the proceeds of crime — including cash structuring patterns — a SAR must be submitted before the account is opened (a "consent SAR" under POCA 2002 s.335).

1.3 CASH STRUCTURING (SMURFING): The deliberate structuring of transactions into amounts below reporting thresholds to avoid detection is an offence under POCA 2002. Three or more consecutive cash deposits each below £10,000 in a single account, particularly where the account holder has relatively modest employment income, constitutes a structuring pattern requiring AML analyst review.

1.4 TIPPING OFF: Under POCA 2002 s.333A, it is a criminal offence to tip off a customer that a SAR has been or may be submitted, or that an AML investigation is underway. Decline notices in structuring cases must not reference AML concerns. Use generic adverse data language only.

1.5 CONSENT AND MORATORIUM: Where a consent SAR is submitted, the firm must await NCA response before proceeding. The NCA has 7 working days to refuse consent. Where consent is refused, the transaction must not proceed. Where no response is received within 7 working days, the firm may proceed.

SECTION 2: MLR 2017 — Money Laundering Regulations

2.1 PEP DEFINITION: A Politically Exposed Person (PEP) is an individual who holds or has held a prominent public function within the last 12 months, including: heads of state, government ministers, members of parliament, senior judicial officials, senior military officials, senior executives of state-owned enterprises, and ambassadors. The definition extends to immediate family members and known close associates.

2.2 EDD OBLIGATION: For all PEP relationships, Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) is mandatory regardless of perceived risk level. Standard CDD is insufficient. EDD must include: (a) senior management approval before account opening, (b) adequate measures to establish the source of wealth and source of funds, and (c) enhanced ongoing monitoring.

2.3 FALSE POSITIVE PEP MATCHES: Where an automated PEP screening match occurs but the firm believes it may be a false positive (e.g. a common name/DOB combination), the firm must still complete EDD before proceeding. The false positive determination must be documented by a compliance officer — it cannot be made unilaterally by the underwriter.

2.4 SOURCE OF FUNDS: For PEP cases, the source of funds must be established to a higher standard than standard CDD. Acceptable evidence includes: SA302 tax returns, audited business accounts, payslips from the public role, or legal documents evidencing inheritance or property sale.

2.5 SENIOR MANAGEMENT APPROVAL: Under MLR 2017, senior management approval is required before opening an account for a confirmed PEP. This approval must be documented and retained for the duration of the relationship and for 5 years thereafter.

SECTION 3: CIFAS Rules and Obligations

3.1 CIFAS CATEGORY 6 — MISUSE OF FACILITY: A CIFAS Category 6 marker indicates that the individual has previously used a financial facility in a way that caused financial loss or constituted fraud (e.g. obtaining credit by deception, using a product for money laundering, or deliberately defaulting while concealing assets). This is the most serious CIFAS category for credit decisions.

3.2 ACTIVE VS DISCHARGED: An active CIFAS Category 6 marker is a hard decline indicator. A discharged CIFAS Category 6 marker (where the prescribed period has elapsed) is not an automatic decline but requires senior underwriter sign-off and documented rationale for any approval.

3.3 CIFAS MEMBER OBLIGATIONS: As a CIFAS member, the firm is obligated to file new markers where fraud or misuse is confirmed. Failure to file a marker where one is warranted is a breach of CIFAS membership rules.

3.4 CATEGORIES 1–5: Lower CIFAS categories (1–5) cover application fraud, misrepresentation, and identity fraud. These require case-by-case assessment. A Category 1 (first-party fraud) marker in combination with other risk signals is typically a decline. Category 3 (misrepresentation) may be manageable depending on age and context.

SECTION 4: National Hunter

4.1 PURPOSE: National Hunter is a shared application fraud database used by UK lenders to identify multiple simultaneous or sequential credit applications that may indicate fraud. A "hit" means the applicant has applied for similar credit at another lender within the lookback window.

4.2 SINGLE HIT: A single National Hunter hit requires investigation. The underwriter should check whether the other application used the same identity details and whether it was declined. A single hit with no other adverse signals may be explained by shopping behaviour.

4.3 MULTIPLE HITS: Two or more National Hunter hits within 6 months is a significant fraud indicator, particularly where different postcodes or name variants are used. This pattern is consistent with sequential application fraud — attempting to obtain credit from multiple lenders before any can share negative data. Refer to senior underwriter.

4.4 HUNTER PLUS CIFAS: The combination of National Hunter hits and a CIFAS marker (any category) is a high-risk signal requiring MLRO involvement, regardless of the age or status of the CIFAS marker.

SECTION 5: Identity Verification

5.1 KNOWLEDGE-BASED AUTHENTICATION (KBA): The firm uses knowledge-based authentication as a first-line identity check. KBA failure does not automatically disqualify an application but should be followed by documentary verification.

5.2 DOCUMENT CHECKS: Document verification (passport, driving licence) provides stronger identity assurance than KBA alone. Where both KBA and document checks pass, identity is considered verified.

5.3 ADDRESS CONSISTENCY: The application address must be consistent with the bureau address and electoral roll. A mismatch between application postcode and bureau postcode is a material fraud indicator, particularly in combination with other signals.

5.4 MULTIPLE ADDRESS CHANGES: Three or more address changes within 24 months, combined with other adverse signals, may indicate an attempt to build a synthetic credit profile or evade previous adverse data.
